# AI Use and Disclosure Policy

This policy governs the use and disclosure of AI assistance in all outputs of `mdfy-ai-research-facility`. It implements §7 of the Charter and is fully aligned with current ICMJE recommendations, Nature and Science editorial policies, and the LLM-use policies of the major ML venues (NeurIPS, ICML, ICLR) as of 2024–2026.

## Principles

1. AI systems are never authors. This is non-negotiable. AI cannot satisfy the ICMJE accountability criterion and therefore cannot be listed in the byline of any unit publication.

2. AI use is always disclosed. The default disclosure standard is upgraded, not minimized.

3. Human authors take full accountability for any AI-assisted output. "Claude said it" is never a defense against an error of fact, methodology, or attribution in a unit publication.

4. AI use is archived. The prompts and outputs that materially shaped a publication are preserved alongside the publication, in a form a reviewer or replicator can inspect.

## Disclosure tiers

The required disclosure scales with the depth of AI contribution.

### Tier 1 — Editorial assistance only

AI was used for grammar, spelling, sentence-level rephrasing, or formatting only. No substantive content was generated by AI.

**Disclosure:** A single sentence in the Acknowledgments naming the AI system used.

> *Example:* "We acknowledge the use of Anthropic's Claude (model: claude-opus-4-6) for copy-editing of the manuscript. All substantive content is the work of the human author(s)."

### Tier 2 — Drafting assistance

AI generated draft text that was then reviewed, revised, and approved by a human author. AI did not generate analysis, figures, or data.

**Disclosure:** A paragraph in the Acknowledgments naming the AI system, the sections it drafted, and the human author who reviewed and approved each section.

### Tier 3 — Execution assistance (analytical or methodological)

AI executed analytical, code, figure-generation, or methodological-implementation work that the human author specified and directed. The human decided what to analyze, how to analyze it, what argument to make; the AI implemented. AI output is graded against a standard the human held before asking.

This is the level at which most of the unit's current work operates.

*Examples:* AI wrote the analysis script for a pre-registered test; AI generated figures from a human-specified plotting specification; AI drafted a Methods section from human-supplied notes; AI implemented a tokenizer ablation the human had already designed.

**Disclosure:** A subsection of Methods titled "AI Assistance" specifying:

- the AI system(s) used (name, provider, model version, date range of use);
- each operation the AI contributed to (analysis, code, figures, etc.);
- the prompting strategy in summary;
- the location of the archived prompts and outputs (Zenodo DOI);
- the human author who is accountable for each AI-contributed element;
- any known limitations or biases of the AI system relevant to the operation.

### Tier 4 — Substantial generation (intellectual origination)

AI originated substantive intellectual content that the human author did not specify in advance and would not have produced unaided. Novel hypotheses, original analytical interpretations, framings, arguments, or methodological choices that the human elected to accept into the work.

The test that distinguishes Tier 4 from Tier 3 is *direction of intellectual origin*, not size of contribution. A bounded AI-originated interpretation that appears in the Discussion is Tier 4; an unbounded AI-executed analysis the human designed is Tier 3.

*Examples:* AI proposed a hypothesis the human had not considered and the human ran with it; AI's interpretation of residuals appears verbatim in the Discussion; AI selected which of several possible analyses to feature in the manuscript; AI generated the framing argument for the Introduction.

**Disclosure:** Tier 3 disclosure plus an explicit accountability statement signed by a human author for each AI-originated element (naming the element, identifying the human author taking accountability under ICMJE criterion 4, and stating that the human reviewed and endorses the AI-originated content as their own published claim), and a section in Discussion addressing the role of AI in the work's contribution.

## Standard disclosure paragraph (Tier 3)

The unit's standard Methods paragraph for Tier 3 disclosure, to be customized per publication:

> *AI Assistance.* This work used Anthropic's Claude (model: \[MODEL\]) between \[START DATE\] and \[END DATE\] to assist with \[OPERATIONS — e.g., code generation, exploratory analysis scripting, manuscript drafting, literature triage\]. The full archive of prompts and substantive AI outputs is deposited at \[ZENODO DOI\]. \[HUMAN AUTHOR\] reviewed all AI-generated content and is accountable for its accuracy and appropriateness. No AI system is listed as an author of this work; the ICMJE authorship criteria require accountability that AI systems cannot bear.

## Archive requirements

For every Tier 3 and Tier 4 publication, the publication folder contains `ai-use-disclosure.md` with:

- the full disclosure paragraph as it appears in the manuscript;
- the model and version used, with the API or release-channel identifier where available;
- the complete prompt archive: every prompt that materially shaped the published work, in chronological order, with the AI's response, in a form a third party could inspect;
- the prompt-engineering strategy in summary;
- a note on any deletions from the archive (e.g., prompts containing personally identifying information of third parties) with the rationale.

The archive is uploaded to Zenodo with the rest of the reproducibility bundle and receives its own DOI or shares the bundle's DOI, at the founder's discretion.

## Internal review of AI-assisted work

When internal review (Gate 6) is itself performed by an AI system, this fact is disclosed in every paper that review touches. The current v0 disclosure language is:

> *Internal Review.* Internal review of this manuscript against the unit's tier-specific standards was performed by \[AI SYSTEM, MODEL VERSION\] under a structured review prompt (committed to the unit's repository at \[PATH\]), and counter-signed by \[HUMAN REVIEWER\]. This arrangement reflects v0 of the unit's governance and is acknowledged as a limitation; see Charter §16.

This disclosure is replaced by a stronger statement once human reviewers are available.

## Exclusions

AI use does not require disclosure when it is entirely incidental to the research and the manuscript: e.g., autocomplete in a code editor for boilerplate, generic web search, spell-check. The threshold for "incidental" is conservative; if in doubt, disclose.

## Updates

This policy is reviewed annually with the Charter and immediately following any change in ICMJE or major-venue policy. Substantial changes appear in `CHANGELOG.md`.

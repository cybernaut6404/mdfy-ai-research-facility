> *The Scoping Memo is Gate 2 of the unit's workflow (Charter §5). It is committed before pre-registration. Its purpose is to make the project's shape explicit early enough that scope creep, hidden risks, and venue mis-targeting can be caught before commitment.*

---

## 1. The question

State the question or contribution in one sentence.

## 2. Why it matters

Two to four sentences. Who would care about a clean answer, and what would they do with it.

## 3. Anticipated tier and justification

State the tier and explain why this tier is appropriate. If the project might span tiers (e.g., a Tier-E pilot followed by a Tier-C confirmation), describe the structure.

## 4. Population, materials, dependencies

What population (humans, models, datasets) the study uses. What materials (instruments, prompts, model checkpoints) are required. What external dependencies must be available (APIs, compute, third-party datasets).

## 5. Anticipated outcomes

Sketch the possible outcomes and what each would mean. This is not a pre-registration; it is an honest preview of the decision tree.

## 6. Anticipated venue

State the intended publication venue or preprint server, with rationale. The venue is recorded here, not chosen post hoc.

## 7. Effort estimate

Rough hours/days and rough compute cost.

## 8. Risks

Substantive risks to the work, in order of likelihood-times-severity. Include at least: data availability, methodological validity, reviewer skepticism, internal capacity, ethical concerns.

## 9. Dual-use and responsible-release assessment

Could this research, if released as planned, materially uplift a malicious actor? State the answer and reasoning. If anything other than a confident no: trigger a Responsible-Release Review before any external release (Charter §11).

## 10. AI-use preview

Which AI systems will be used, for what operations, at what tier (per `AI_USE_POLICY.md`).

## 11. COI preview

Any known conflicts of interest that bear on this project.

- [ ] Hold pending [REASON]
- [ ] Abandon
